# Built4U client copy review — October 12, 2026

This review applies Customer Acquisition Tools-B2C's Website Copy Analyzer to the three short service descriptions. Context was already supplied: Built4U at https://customtechbuilt4u.com/, custom website/app/software development, and prospective business owners or teams. The exact approved opening headline, tagline, and body are preserved. Possible buyer concerns below are interpretations, not audience research or measured results.

## Website service

**Original copy:** “Present your business clearly, with mobile-friendly pages and content you can update yourself.”

| Possible prospect problem | Feature statement | Benefit | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visitors may struggle to understand the business or find the next step. | Mobile-friendly pages and editable content. | Present the business clearly and keep information current; both are already in the copy. | Suggested addition: visitors can understand the offer and contact the business. This is a design objective, not a guaranteed increase in leads. |

**Analysis:** The copy is clear but does not pair the service with a visible example or a concrete visitor action.

**Recommended copy:** “A website that explains what you do,” “*with a clear path for customers to contact you or make a booking*,” “**so the next step is easy to find**.”

## Web application service

**Original copy:** “Replace spreadsheets and disconnected tools with accounts, bookings, approvals, and payments in one app.”

| Possible prospect problem | Feature statement | Benefit | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Work may be spread across separate tools and spreadsheets. | Accounts, bookings, approvals, and payments in one app. | A connected workflow, already expressed by the original copy. | Suggested addition: a single place to complete and track the chosen tasks. No time or cost saving is asserted. |

**Analysis:** This is the strongest existing problem-to-feature connection. A genuine example should make the scope tangible.

**Recommended copy:** “Bring bookings, approvals, and payments into one app,” “*built around how your team works*,” “**with a connected place to manage the work**.”

## App service

**Original copy:** “Create an iPhone app for your customers or team, with native features and App Store delivery.”

| Possible prospect problem | Feature statement | Benefit | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The business may have an idea that needs a usable app. | Native iPhone development and App Store delivery. | Suggested addition: turn the intended workflow into an app customers or staff can use. | Suggested addition: move from an idea to a working product. Distribution, functionality, and project scope must be agreed; no acceptance guarantee is implied. |

**Analysis:** The original describes the platform more clearly than the buyer's reason for building it. Real native app examples provide concrete evidence.

**Recommended copy:** “Turn your idea into an app,” “*designed around what your customers or team need to do*,” “**from the first screen to a working product**.”

## Application to the site

The implementation uses concise business scenarios with genuine project examples, a visible scope/build/launch sequence, and short answers about starting with an unfinished idea, scope-based pricing, and discussing ongoing responsibilities. Added review wording above is shown in quotation marks; benefits are italicized and proposed outcomes bolded for traceability. The website itself uses ordinary readable typography.

No customer relationships, testimonials, metrics, results, fixed prices, delivery dates, free ongoing support, ownership terms, or special guarantees were invented. The two earlier public business-owner discussions were directional examples, not demand validation. The next step is implementation and functional/visual verification, already authorized by the user.
